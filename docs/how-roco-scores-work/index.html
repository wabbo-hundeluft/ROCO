Three scores. Three different questions.

ROCO's scores are local wellness estimates, not official Google, Fitbit, WHOOP or Garmin scores. The published implementation is the reference: model health-coach-v2.2, weekly model adaptive-3/weekly. These are design rules, not clinically validated formulas.

Body energy: what is left in my day?

On a 0–100 scale, the model starts from available recovery signals: sleep basis 20%, HRV 50%, resting heart rate 30%, reweighted if a permitted optional signal is missing. Current HRV and a usable personal comparison are required. Sleep debt and a short-night cap can reduce starting energy.

Awake time, recorded workouts and steps outside overlapping workouts then subtract energy. Wake time uses a model assumption of two points per hour plus an extra point per hour after 12 awake hours. This is not continuous measured stress. Elapsed time alone does not refund used energy. A high prior weekly total is not directly deducted again from morning physiology.

The 0–100 result uses available components. Fewer than two usable components prevents a result. Regularity needs at least five prior nights; optional missing components make the score provisional. The result is capped at duration-component + 15 points so good physiology cannot erase a very short night. Stages are estimated by the source device and are not a laboratory measurement.

Weekly strain: how much of my weekly target have I used?

Weekly points = weighted load so far ÷ personal weekly target × 100

Example only: 22 weighted units against a 220-unit target gives 10 points. This illustrates normalization, not a universal recommended target. Heart-rate-weighted duration differs from raw cardio minutes. Insufficient pulse coverage uses a labelled duration approximation.

100 means your agreed/adaptive weekly target; it is not perfect health. Values above 100 mean extra load, not proof of overtraining. The target is derived from past weeks, short- and long-term load trends and available recovery context; today's workout does not move this week's baseline target. Calendar weeks are compared separately.

Personal context, updates and uncertainty

Optional age, activity and heart-rate settings can influence applicable goals and model parameters. Personal baselines are preferred to a universal HRV cutoff. Automatic refresh is throttled to about one hour; manual refresh can request a new calculation. Import success, source timestamps and permissions still matter. A fallback saved score remains visibly dated.

Missing data, denied permission, incomplete imports, stale nights and source conflicts have distinct states. Never treat an unavailable score as zero or a provisional score as certainty.
